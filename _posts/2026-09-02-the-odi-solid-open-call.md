The [Open Data Institute](https://theodi.org/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}
(ODI) has launched an
[open call](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/solid-open-call/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}
to speed up the adoption of [Solid](https://solidproject.org/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}
among non-commercial organisations. It plans to pick six projects that use
Solid for social good, and to support each of them for six months.

## The call in brief

{% include logo.html src="/assets/images/solid-logo.svg" alt="The Solid logo" %}

**What Solid is.** Solid is an open standard for digital identity and
personal data. Instead of every app keeping its own copy of your data, you
keep it in a personal online data store, a *Pod*, and decide which apps and
people can use it. It came out of Sir Tim Berners-Lee's lab at MIT, is
incubated by the W3C Solid Community Group, and is being standardised by the
Linked Web Storage Working Group. There's more on this site's
[What is Solid?](/what-is-solid/) page.

**Who can apply.** Only non-commercial projects: non-profits, charities,
academic institutions, civil society groups and individuals. Projects that
aim to make a profit from Solid aren't eligible. No prior Solid experience
is needed, and there's no required team size; individuals are welcome. The
ODI wants a cohort that is diverse in size, maturity, sector and place, with
at least two projects from outside Europe and North America.

**How projects are judged.** A panel of four judges, three from the Solid
Advisory Committee and one from the ODI, will look at technical
compatibility, potential for impact, clarity of the proposed solution and
likelihood of success. The ODI checks eligibility first.

**The timeline.**

| Date | Milestone |
| - | - |
| 1st September | Applications open |
| 15th September | Solid World, with more about the call |
| 30th September, 17:00 BST | Applications close |
| 1st–16th October | Judging |
| 19th–30th October | Interviews |
| 2nd November | Six months of work begin, ending 30th April 2027 |

**What selected projects get.**

- **Pod hosting.** Pods on ODI-hosted servers, both Inrupt's Enterprise
  Solid Server and an open-source one, as a testbed for developing and
  piloting. They're not for sensitive data, though the ODI can advise on
  options for that later.
- **Strategic support.** Two workshops: one on how Solid fits the project's
  area and architecture, and one on an implementation roadmap. The ODI will
  also put projects in touch with others in the Solid ecosystem.
- **Advocacy.** Help explaining the project's plans and results, through
  blogs, webinars and ODI communications. Projects are expected to take part
  in some of these.
- **Technical support.** Applicants choose the tier that fits them best:
  *Start* (a new idea and a proof of concept), *Refine* (an existing
  prototype heading for a pilot) or *Scale* (an established project heading
  for production). Engineering time from the ODI is then shared out by need,
  across work specific to one project, reusable work for a project's domain
  (such as data models), and improvements to generic open-source Solid
  tools.
- **Reporting.** Six months after the support period ends, each project
  reports on its outcomes, and the ODI writes up the call as a whole.

## What I'd like to do

{% include logo.html src="/assets/images/code-club-logo.svg" alt="The Code Club logo" %}

I run a local [Code Club](https://codeclub.org/en/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"},
where children learn to code, mostly with
[Scratch](https://scratch.mit.edu/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}. Our
club's site is here:
[gravesend-code-club.viboko.dev](https://gravesend-code-club.viboko.dev/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"}.

I would like to explore the possibility of modifying Scratch to add the
ability to store projects to a Solid pod. As it stands the permissions and
consent model used by Scratch is fairly primitive. A child's projects could
live in a Pod of their own, and the child and their parents could decide who
gets to see them. It feels like there is plenty of potential! My plan is to
spend some time investigating and try to get an application together...
watch this space!
